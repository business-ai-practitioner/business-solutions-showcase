# Management intelligence

## Purpose

Turn individual quality findings into information that helps managers improve investigation practice, controls, training and procedures.

A count of errors alone is rarely enough. Managers need to know what is going wrong, why it matters, where it occurs and what action could address it.

## Example quality themes

| Theme | Example issue | Potential management response |
|---|---|---|
| Counterparty understanding | Relationship with payer or beneficiary not established | Refresh guidance and examples for counterparty enquiries |
| Source and purpose | Explanation recorded but not supported | Improve evidence standards and coaching |
| Transaction coverage | Relevant payments omitted from narrative | Clarify transaction review expectations |
| Chronology | Contact and payment sequence unclear in fraud cases | Standardise incident chronology |
| Evidence traceability | Conclusion not linked to source record | Improve case recording and reviewer challenge |
| Outcome reasoning | Final position does not address unresolved gaps | Reinforce conclusion-writing standards |

## Suggested management view

A mature implementation could report:
- Number of cases reviewed by investigation type.
- Findings by theme and severity.
- Repeat findings by team or process, where appropriate and fair.
- Age of open corrective actions.
- Rework and repeat-review trends.
- Training or procedure changes completed.
- Whether the same issue is recurring after intervention.

## Interpretation safeguards

- Define each quality theme consistently before comparing periods or teams.
- Distinguish case counts from finding counts.
- Consider case complexity and sample selection.
- Do not compare teams without considering workload and case mix.
- Do not treat a small or targeted sample as representative.
- Protect customer and staff confidentiality.
- Do not use the prototype's three sample cases as a performance benchmark.

## What is shown in this project

The prototype groups predefined checklist flags across three synthetic examples. It is a demonstration of the type of categorisation a management view could use. It is not a live dashboard, statistically meaningful trend analysis or evidence of improved performance.

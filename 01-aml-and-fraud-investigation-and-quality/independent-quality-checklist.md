# Independent quality review checklist

## How to use this checklist

The reviewer should read the case information and investigation reasoning independently. The checklist is a challenge tool, not a substitute for judgement.

For each item, record:
- **Pass:** adequately addressed and supported.
- **Needs improvement:** partly addressed, unclear or weakly supported.
- **Fail:** material omission or unsupported reasoning.
- **Not applicable:** genuinely irrelevant to this case, with a reason.

A reviewer should record the evidence supporting the finding and the correction required. Do not create an arbitrary overall score unless the bank has defined, calibrated and tested a scoring methodology.

## AML review

| Review area | Reviewer question |
|---|---|
| Customer profile | Has the investigator considered the relevant customer and account profile? |
| Transaction coverage | Have the material incoming and outgoing payments been considered? |
| Payment parties | Are relevant payers and beneficiaries identified and their relationships examined? |
| Source of funds | Has the origin of relevant funds been investigated rather than assumed? |
| Purpose of payments | Has the purpose of incoming and outgoing activity been assessed? |
| Customer explanation | Is the explanation recorded accurately and tested against available evidence? |
| Flow of funds | Is onward movement considered where relevant? |
| Evidence | Are material statements and conclusions linked to appropriate sources? |
| Reasoning | Does the narrative explain why the activity is or is not understood? |
| Gaps and outcome | Are unresolved issues and the provisional case position consistent? |

## Fraud review

| Review area | Reviewer question |
|---|---|
| Incident narrative | Is the customer's account of what happened recorded clearly? |
| Chronology | Are contact, payment, discovery and reporting times set out? |
| Deception | Has the alleged deception or impersonation been examined? |
| Authorisation | Is payment authorisation distinguished from the question of deception? |
| Payment details | Are amounts, dates, channels and beneficiaries accurately recorded? |
| Fund movement | Have available onward movement details been considered? |
| Customer impact | Is the reported loss distinguished from confirmed loss or recovered funds? |
| Response | Are relevant bank response and recovery steps recorded? |
| Evidence | Are material facts and statements sourced and distinguished? |
| Outcome | Is the case position supported and consistent with outstanding enquiries? |

## Cross-cutting review

- Are facts, statements, observations, inferences and unknowns clearly distinguished?
- Does the conclusion go beyond the evidence?
- Are alternative explanations considered where relevant?
- Are material gaps explained, not merely listed?
- Are recommended enquiries specific and proportionate?
- Is the investigation record understandable to another investigator?
- Is the quality finding clear about impact and required correction?

## Writing a useful quality finding

**Weak finding:** "Counterparty analysis incomplete."

**Stronger finding:** "The investigation records three incoming payments but does not establish the relationship between the customer and the payers. This limits the assessment of the stated repayment explanation. Establish and record the relationship for each payer, and assess whether the available supporting information is consistent with the explanation."

A good finding contains:
1. The issue.
2. Why it matters.
3. The evidence or missing evidence.
4. The corrective action.
5. The expected closure evidence.

## Independence

The reviewer should not simply accept the investigator's conclusion or an automated prompt. The reviewer must assess the underlying evidence and reasoning. The investigator remains responsible for responding to findings; the reviewer remains responsible for the independent assessment.

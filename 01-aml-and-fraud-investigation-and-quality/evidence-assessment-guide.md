# Evidence Assessment Guide

## Purpose

Prevent a case narrative from presenting an explanation, inference or assumption as an established fact.

## Evidence classifications

| Classification | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verified fact | Supported by a reliable source that has been checked | A transaction appears in the supplied bank transaction extract |
| Customer explanation | What the customer says happened or why | Customer states a payment was for a vehicle |
| Inference | A reasoned interpretation of available facts | Several payments followed by transfers may indicate pass-through activity |
| Assumption | A proposition not yet supported by evidence | The unrelated senders are business customers |
| Unknown | Information not established in the available case material | Beneficial relationship with the recipients |

## Minimum evidence register fields

- Evidence ID.
- Issue or assertion addressed.
- Source type and source reference.
- Date obtained.
- Summary of what it establishes.
- Verification status.
- Reliability considerations.
- Contradictory evidence.
- Related transaction or event.
- Further enquiry.

## AML considerations

- Customer profile and expected activity.
- Identity and relationship of counterparties.
- Source of funds and, where relevant, source of wealth.
- Purpose of incoming and outgoing payments.
- Movement and destination of funds.
- Customer explanation and independent corroboration.
- Alternative explanations and contradictory evidence.

## Fraud considerations

- Reported fraud or scam type.
- Event chronology.
- How contact or deception allegedly occurred.
- Whether the payment was authorised and what the customer understood.
- Beneficiary and receiving-account information available to the investigator.
- Subsequent movement of funds where available.
- Customer, bank, merchant or third-party evidence.
- Contradictory or missing information.

## Required discipline

A customer explanation is not independently verified merely because it has been recorded. An unusual transaction is not, by itself, proof of criminal conduct. A missing document is an evidence gap, not proof that the underlying event did not occur.

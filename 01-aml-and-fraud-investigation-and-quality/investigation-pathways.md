# Two investigation pathways: AML and fraud

The project deliberately does not use one generic checklist for every financial crime case. AML and fraud investigations may share information and controls, but the questions they need to answer are different.

## AML investigation

**Core question:** Does the customer's activity make sense in the context of what the bank knows, and can the source and purpose of funds be reasonably understood?

| Area | Questions to consider |
|---|---|
| Customer profile | What is known about the customer's occupation, income, business and expected account use? |
| Incoming payments | Who paid the customer, how often and in what amounts? |
| Counterparties | What is the relationship between the customer and each payer? |
| Source of funds | Where did the money originate, and what evidence supports that explanation? |
| Outgoing payments | Who received the money, and what was the stated purpose? |
| Flow of funds | Was money transferred onward soon after receipt? What does that show—and what does it not show? |
| Customer explanation | Is the explanation specific, consistent and supported by independent information? |
| Outstanding enquiries | What information would help resolve the material gaps? |

A pattern can justify further enquiries without establishing money laundering or other criminal conduct.

## Fraud investigation

**Core question:** What happened to the customer or account, how were the payments made, who received the funds, and what can be done under the bank's process to investigate or respond?

| Area | Questions to consider |
|---|---|
| Scam story | What did the customer say happened, and when? |
| Contact and deception | Who contacted the customer, through which channel, and what were they told? |
| Payment chronology | When were payments created, authorised, processed and reported? |
| Authorisation | Did the customer make or approve the payment? Was the customer deceived, coerced or impersonated? |
| Beneficiary | Who received the funds? What is known and what remains unverified? |
| Fund movement | Were funds moved onward? What information is available from the bank's records or enquiries? |
| Customer impact | What loss is reported, what amount is confirmed, and what remains under review? |
| Response and recovery | What contact, recall, hold or recovery steps were taken under the bank's procedures? |

A payment being authorised by a customer does not, by itself, establish that the customer was not deceived or that the payment was legitimate.

## Shared evidence and quality layer

Both pathways use a common discipline:
- Separate bank-record facts from customer statements.
- Identify inferences and assumptions.
- Record unknowns rather than filling gaps with invented information.
- Link material observations to their source.
- Keep the provisional position proportionate to the evidence.
- Have an independent reviewer challenge the work.

## Why this distinction matters

A generic prompt such as "check the source of funds" may be relevant in an AML case but is not enough to investigate an authorised payment scam. Fraud work needs a clear chronology, the deception story, payment authorisation and fund-recovery enquiries.

Likewise, an AML case should not be reduced to whether the customer reported a scam. It needs a reasoned assessment of activity, counterparties, source and purpose.

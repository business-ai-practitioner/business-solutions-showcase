# Publication Boundaries and IP Approach

## Public showcase

The public repository is intended to demonstrate the business problem, product experience and intended capabilities.

Suitable public material:
- product proposition and high-level workflow;
- synthetic scenarios and illustrative outputs;
- screenshots and interface walkthrough;
- high-level architecture and limitations;
- design principles and governance boundaries.

## Keep private during development

Do not publish commercially sensitive implementation material merely to make the repository appear more complete. Keep the following in a private development location unless a deliberate release decision is made:
- production system instructions and prompt chains;
- detailed assessment and validation rules;
- proprietary rating-calibration methods;
- detailed exception handling and orchestration;
- private evaluation datasets and test cases;
- credentials, service configuration and integration details.

## Important practical limitations

- A public browser application exposes its front-end source. Do not place secrets or sensitive logic in client-side code.
- A private repository reduces access but is not, by itself, a complete IP protection strategy.
- Copyright generally protects original expression, not the general business idea or functionality. Obtain appropriate legal advice before commercial release or licensing.
- Do not use employer-confidential information, proprietary rating frameworks or real employee data.
- Do not imply that confidentiality labels prevent copying once material has been published publicly.

## Publication checklist

- [ ] All employee names and data are fictional.
- [ ] No employer names, internal frameworks or confidential material appear.
- [ ] No API keys, credentials or private endpoints are included.
- [ ] Concept, prototype and tested functionality are accurately distinguished.
- [ ] No unsupported ROI, accuracy or productivity claims are made.
- [ ] No third-party branding or proprietary materials are used without permission.
- [ ] Decide deliberately whether to add an open-source licence. Without a licence, default copyright restrictions generally remain; do not assume public visibility grants reuse rights.

## Positioning principle

**Showcase the capability. Protect the implementation. Demonstrate the value.**

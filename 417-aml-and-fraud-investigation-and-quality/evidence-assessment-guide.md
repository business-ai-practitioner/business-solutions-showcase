# Evidence assessment guide

## Purpose

Help investigators and reviewers distinguish what is known from what is said, inferred or still unknown. The aim is not to produce more text. It is to make the reasoning easier to challenge.

## Five evidence labels

| Label | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Recorded fact | Information directly shown in an identified bank record or reliable source | Bank transaction record shows a transfer of AUD 6,000 on 5 September |
| Customer statement | What the customer has told the bank; not independently verified merely because it was recorded | Customer says the incoming payments were repayments from friends |
| Observation | A description of what the available information shows | Three incoming payments were received over two days |
| Inference | A possible explanation drawn from facts; it needs reasoning and may be wrong | The rapid onward transfers may indicate the customer was moving funds for another party |
| Unknown / evidence gap | Information not established by the current material | Relationship with the payers has not been verified |

Do not label an inference as a fact. Do not label a customer statement as verified evidence unless it has been independently corroborated.

## A practical evidence record

For each material issue, record:

1. **Question:** What are we trying to establish?
2. **Information available:** What record, statement or observation is relevant?
3. **Source:** Where did the information come from?
4. **What it establishes:** What can reasonably be said?
5. **What it does not establish:** What should not be assumed?
6. **Gap:** What remains unknown?
7. **Next enquiry:** What proportionate step could help resolve it?

## Example — AML

**Question:** What is the source of the incoming funds?

- Available information: account records show payments from three individuals.
- Customer statement: customer says these are repayments of personal loans.
- What this establishes: the customer has provided an explanation.
- What it does not establish: that the payers borrowed money from the customer or that the funds originated from a legitimate source.
- Gap: no loan records or independent confirmation have been provided.
- Next enquiry: establish the relationship with each payer and seek proportionate supporting information.

## Example — fraud

**Question:** Did the customer make the payment, and was the customer deceived?

- Available information: bank records show the customer authenticated three payments.
- Customer statement: customer says a caller claiming to be from the bank instructed them to move funds to a "safe account".
- What this establishes: the payments were authenticated using the recorded method; the customer has described a suspected impersonation scam.
- What it does not establish: the identity of the caller, the beneficiary's role or the final destination of the funds.
- Gap: contact details, beneficiary information and onward movement need further examination.
- Next enquiry: preserve relevant contact records and follow the bank's fraud response and recovery procedures.

## Writing a balanced case position

A useful case position should:
- State the material facts.
- Explain the important observations.
- Acknowledge contrary or missing information.
- Explain why the evidence is or is not sufficient.
- Identify outstanding enquiries.
- Avoid definitive language where the evidence is incomplete.

The prototype does not make this judgement. The guide describes the standard a human investigator should apply.
